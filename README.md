# Detection-of-Oral-Cancer-Derived-Extracellular-Vesicles-Using-an-AIassisted-Acoustofluidic-Biosensor
Oral Cancer Biosensor

This paper presents a shear-horizontal surface acoustic wave (SH-SAW) biosensor integrated with a machine learning (ML) framework for non-invasive, isolation-free detection of oral squamous cell carcinoma (OSCC) using salivary exosomal biomarkers. Fig. 1 illustrates the conceptual work aiming to achieve in the project. The work addresses delayed diagnosis bottlenecks and high mortality rates associated with traditional, invasive diagnostic methods. To establish an optimized panel, a stability-driven ML model utilizing feature selection analyzed surface-accessible proteins from CPTAC head and neck cancer datasets. This process identified a three-protein biomarker signature consisting of CD44, CD9, and CD59, which yielded a theoretical receiver operating characteristic area under the curve (AUC) of 0.97. The label-free SH-SAW biosensor measures acoustic phase shifts from microfluidic surface-binding events. It operates using 5 µL of saliva samples with an assay time under 30 min, bypassing complex exosome isolation protocols. In characterization using CAL-27 cell-derived exosomes, the biosensor achieved a limit of detection of 1.4×107 #/mL for CD44, with a linear dynamic range spanning 4×106 to 4×1010 #/mL. Clinical evaluation across 58 human participants (35 OSCC patients, 12 with oral potentially malignant disorders, and 11 healthy controls) was analyzed using an unsupervised K-means clustering model. The integrated three-marker panel achieved a clinical classification accuracy of 91.7% and a specificity of 95.8%. Individual ROC analysis showed standalone CD44 attained an AUC of 0.98 for OSCC detection. In a subsequent single-blinded clinical study of six patients, the platform achieved 100% accuracy and 100% sensitivity. This success paves the way for more reliable cancer screening in the near future.
